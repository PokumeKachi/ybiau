## Introduction to physics

    - Classical mechanics

    - Thermodynamics
    - Electromagnetism

    - Quantum mechanics

### Classical mechanics

- This field concerns the motion of objects that are large relative to atom (too small particles follow the rules of Quantum Mechanics instead) and very slow relative to the speed of light (too fast objects follow the rules of Special Relativity)
    - Also too dense objects (objects with too strong gravitational fields like black holes, the early Universe) follow the rules of General Relativity instead

# Measurement

- Classical mechanics aims to predict qualitatively and quantitatively the results of experiments.
- In order to be quantitative, we need to have measurements for quantities.
- Measurements consist of: Number + Unit
    - For example: 2.29 meters, 141 kg
- Physical quantities have units assigned to them (Mass has lb, kg, length has cm, ft,...).
- Each quantity has a dimension.

- The SI unit standards define 7 base quantities, their units are base units, meaning their dimension consists of a base dimension, a single dimension with a power of 1.
- Meanwhile most other quantities have their dimensions derived from products of other dimensions with varying powers.
- That sounds confusing but look, let's take Mass (M), Length (L), and Time (T) for example.
    - dim(mass) = M
    - dim(length) = L
    - dim(time) = T
    - dim(speed) = $$\frac{L}T$$
- Sometimes dimensions can cancel out each other resulting in dim(unit) = 1, in which case that unit is dimensionless.

### Dimensional analysis

- Example: Show that the expression $E_k =\frac12mv^2$, where $E_k$ represents kinetic energy, $m$ mass, and $v$ velocity, is dimensionally correct.
    - The dimension of $E_k$ is the dimension of energy
        - Let's try referring to a separate formula, $E_p$, potential energy, also is a type of energy and has the formula $E_p=mgh$
        - Since both kinetic energy and potential energy are energy quantities, they share the same dimension of $[E]=M\cdot \frac{L}{T^2}\cdot L=M\cdot \frac{L^2}{T^2}$
    * The dimension of $\frac12mv^2$ is $[\frac12mv^2]=M\cdot\frac{L^2}{T^2}$
    * Hence the dimensions match, so the formula is dimensionally consistent with energy.

# Dynamics


## Introduction

- Kinematics is a branch of mechanics that is only concerned with describing motion (displacement, velocity, acceleration), but not the cause (force, mass, energy).
- Dynamics is the branch of mechanics that builds upon kinematics by including the causes of motion.

- Constraint (special case) definitions
    * One-dimensional motion: Motion along a straight line
    * Particle model: We are only concerned with describing the object as a particle (a point-like object) regardless of its size (we do not care about its center of mass, rotation,..)
